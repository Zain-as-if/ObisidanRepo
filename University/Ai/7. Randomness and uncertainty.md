Randomness $\ne$ Uncertainty 

Randomness usually applied to situations where range and frequency of outcomes known. 

Uncertainty also results from **lack of knowledge**

A phenomenon may follow some pattern due to some underlying constraints or rules, or because of the strategy of an opponent

If we don't understand why the pattern arises, will be unpredictable (seem random)

Often uncertainty will involve both uncertainty due to randomness and uncertainty due to lack of knowledge

<h4>Minimax with randomness</h4>
Chance events such as dice rolls can be represented within a game tree

Minimax algorithm can be extended to incorporate probability-weighted sums of the values of subtrees below each possible outcome.

Rock-paper-scissors (RPS) actually involves the hard computational problem of **temporal pattern recognition**

Temporal patterns can be modelled by the use of **Markov Models**, widely used in AI and can be directly applied to games such as RPS.

One simple type of Markov Model is the **n-gram** model which can be easily applied to RPS

<h4>N-gram</h4>
n-gram is a sequence of *n* items from some sequence

Items could be numbers, letters, words or some kind of actions

An n-gram model is a frequency distribution over (usually large) set of example sequences. For every combination ($x_{1},\dots,x_{n}$) of items, the frequency that this occurs in the example set is recorded.

This information can be used to determine the probability with which a particular item $x_{n}$ will follow a preceding sequence of n-1 items:$$P(x_{n}|x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})$$
<h4>Pacman</h4>
Each ghost in Pacman is heading for a specific location (on a tiled grid) 

Each of 4 ghosts has a different way of picking its target tile:
- Red Blinky (Pursuer) - Pac-Man's Current Tile
- Pinky (Speed/Ambusher) - 4 Tiles in front of Pac-Man
- Inky (Bashful/Whimsical) - Tile equidistant and opposite to the position of Blinky, relative to the point 2 tiles in front of Pac-Man
- Orange Clyde (Pokey/Feigning Ignorance) - If >8 tiles from Pac-Man, target Pac-Man's current tile, otherwise head to bottom left corner (retreat)

At each intersection, ghost chooses the way to go using the heuristic of **smallest Euclidean distance to goal** (calculated from centre of each possible exit tile)

<h4>Why Pac-Man Starves</h4>
Sometimes looking ahead can result in dallying behaviour, where a beneficial action is never taken because it would always be possible to take it later with the same net benefit

Pac-Man may choose not to move left and eat the energy blip, since it could always move right and eat the other energy blip on the following turn. Also moving right leaves more options open

Reasoning like this will lead to Pac-Man starving
