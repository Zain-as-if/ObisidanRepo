The methodology of Knowledge Representation & Automated Reasoning employs a symbolic representation of information together with logical inference procedures as a means for solving problems

<h4>Methodologies</h4>
Neural Nets: Study the structure & function of the brain & try to recreate or simulate it

Situated & Reactive AI: Observe and seek to simulate intelligent behaviour by modelling the way in which an intelligent agent reacts to its environment

Popular methodology is to look first at simple organisms, such as insects, as a first step towards understanding more high-level intelligence

The KR paradigm takes **language** as an essential vehicle for intelligence

Animals can be seen as semi-intelligent because they only posses a rudimentary form of language, principle role of language is to represent information
<h4>Language & Logic</h4>
Patterns of natural language inference are used as a guide to the form of valid principles of logical deduction

Logical representations clean up natural language and aim to make it more definite

e.g. 
If it is raining, I shall stay in 
It is Raining
Therefore, I shall stay in 

R -> S
R
$\therefore$ S
<h4>Formalisation & Abstraction</h4>
In employing a formal logical representation we aim to abstract from irrelevant details of natural descriptions to arrive at the essential structure of reasoning

Typically even ignore much of the logical structure present in natural language because we are only interested in (or only know) certain modes of reasoning

E.g. for many purposes we can ignore the tense structure of natural language

Reasoning in natural language can be regarded as **semi-formal**

For a "Good" representation:
- must determine what knowledge is relevant to the problem
- need to find a suitable **level of abstraction**
- need a representation language in which problem and solution can adequately be expressed
- need a **correct** formalisation of problem & solution in that language
- need a **logical theory** of the modes of reasoning required to solve the problem

Tough issue that any AI reasoning system must confront is **Tractability** 

Problem domain is intractable if it is not possible for a (conventional) computer program to solve it in 'reasonable' time (and with 'reasonable' use of other resources such as memory)

Certain classes of logical problems are not only intractable but also **undecidable**, means that there is no program that, given any instance of the problem, will in **finite time** either:
1. Find a solution
2. Terminate having determined that no solution exists
<h4>Space Time</h4>
Since mathematical truths are eternal, not geared towards representing temporal information

Knowledge of spatial properties & relationships are required for many common-sense reasoning problems

To solve these common-sense problems, often need detailed knowledge about everyday objects

For many purposes we would like to be able to reason with knowledge involving both spatial and temporal information, e.g. may want to reason about a physical mechanism

Important application for spatial-temporal reasoning is robot control 
Success has been achieved for some constrained environments, flexible solutions are elusive

Versatile high-level control of autonomous agents major goal of KR. Can use logical formulae to give high-level descriptions of robot actions 
<h4>Uncertainty</h4>
Much of information available to intelligent human (or computer) is affected by some degree of uncertainty

Can arise from:
- Unreliable information sources
- Inaccurate measurements
- Out of date information
- Unsound (but perhaps potentially useful) deductions 
Big problem for AI, popular approaches for problems include **probabilistic** & **fuzzy** logics

But ordinary classical logics can mitigate the problem by use of **generality**, e.g. instead of prob($\phi$) = 0.7, might assert a more general claim $\phi \lor \psi$ 
<h4>Ontology</h4>
Structured representation of knowledge that defines:
- Entities
- Relationships
- Categories & Rules

Used to refer to a rigorous logical specification of a domain of objects and the concepts of relationships that apply to the domain 

Ontologies intended to guarantee the coherence of information and to allow reliable exchanges of information between computer systems

Huge problem that obstructs construction of rigorous ontologies is the widespread presence of **ambiguity** and **vagueness** in natural concepts
