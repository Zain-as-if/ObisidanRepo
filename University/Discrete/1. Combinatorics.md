<h4>Product Rule</h4>
Procedure described as sequence of *m* independent steps, with $i_{1}$ possible outcomes for first step, $i_{2}$ possible outcomes for the 2nd step,..., and $i_{m}$possible outcomes for the $m^{th}$ step, then the total number of composite outcomes of the procedure is $i_{1}i_{2}\dots i_{m}$ 

Sequential counting procedure is one that satisfies properties:
1. Steps are **ordered**, i.e. any 2 sequences of different outcomes represents distinguishable composite outcomes
2. Steps are **independent**, i.e. number of outcomes of one step is not affected by the outcomes of the preceding steps 
3. Steps are **complete**, i.e. each composite outcome consists of a complete sequence of individual outcomes, one for each step
E.g.
Binary sequence whose elements come from set {0,1}. For example, 100110 is binary sequence of length 6
a) How binary sequences of length 5 are there?
- 2 outcomes for position 1
- 2 outcomes for position 2
- ...
- 2 outcomes for position 5
- $2\cdot 2\cdot 2\cdot 2\cdot 2 = 2^5 = 32$ 
b) How many binary sequences of length 5 begin with 101?
- 1 outcome for position 1 (1)
- 1 outcome for position 2 (0)
- 1 outcome for position 3 (1)
- 2 outcomes for position 4 (0,1)
- 2 outcomes for position 5 (0,1)
- $1\cdot 1\cdot 1\cdot 2\cdot 2=1^3\cdot 2^2=4$ 

<u>K-Element Sequence</u>
Let $a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k}$ be a k-element sequence or a k-tuple (note order of elements is important). Sometimes k-tuples denoted by $(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k}$). If there are *n* choices for each element of sequence, by Multiplication Principle, number of different k-element sequences is $n^k$

E.g.
Find number of distinct divisors of the integer 64800
64800 = $2^53^45^2$ prime factorisation
Any divisor of 64800 must have prime factorisation of form $2^i3^j5^k$ where $0\leq i\leq 5,0\leq j\leq 4,0\leq k\leq 2$ 
Sequential Counting Procedure: 
1. Choose i (6 choices)
2. Choose j (5 choices) 
3. Choose k (3 choices)
4. Total number of divisors is $6\cdot 5 \cdot 3=90$ 

6 Different books arranged on bookshelf
a) How many distinguishable orders of arrangement are there
Procedure:
1. Choose 1 book for position 1 (6 choices)
2. Choose different book for position 2 (5 choices)
3. ...
4. Choose last book for position 6 (1 choice)
5. $6\cdot 5 \cdot 4\cdot 3\cdot 2\cdot 1=6! =720$

b) Suppose that 2 of 6 books are blue and should not be placed next to each other, how many different arrangements when 2 blue books not adjacent. 
1. Choose 2 consecutive positions for blue books (5 choices)
2. Arrange blue books in these positions (2 choices)
3. Arrange 4 remaining books in remaining positions ($4\cdot 3\cdot 2\cdot 1=24$ ways)
4. Number of arrangements do not want is $5\cdot 2\cdot 24=240$ 
5. Number of arrangements we do want is $720-240=480$
<h4>Sum Rule</h4>
If procedure can be broken up into *m* events or cases whose sets of outcomes are mutually exclusive, with $j_{1}$ possible outcomes for first event, $j_{2}$ possible outcomes for second event,..., and $j_{m}$ possible outcomes for the $m^{th}$ event, then total number of outcomes of procedure is $j_{1}+j_{2}+\dots+j_{m}$ 

E.g.
On bookshelf there are 5 different calculus books, 6  different linear algebra books, 7 different combinatorics books
a) How many ways can a pair of books of different types be chosen
3 mutually exclusive cases can occur:
- Calculus - Linear Algebra pair ($5\cdot 6$ choices)
- Linear Algebra - Combinatorics pair ($6\cdot 7$ choices)
- Calculus - Combinatorics pair ($5\cdot 7$ choices)
- There are $5\cdot 6+6\cdot 7+5\cdot 7=107$ different outcomes
b) How many ways can single book of each type be chosen
Count as follows:
1. Choose calculus book (5 choices)
2. Choose linear algebra book (6 choices)
3. Choose combinatorics book (7 choices)
4. There are $5\cdot 6\cdot 7=210$ 
<h4>Principle Of Inclusion-Exclusion</h4>
Let $A_{1}$ and $A_{2}$ be two sets. Number of ways to select an element from $A_{1}$ is $|A_{1}|$, number of ways to select element from $A_{2}$ is $|A_{2}|$, number of ways to select element from $A_{1}$ or $A_{2}$ is $|A_{1}\lor A_{2}|=|A_{1}|+|A_{2}|-|A_{1} \cap A_{2}|$
